Pipeline Step 5 — DFM

Design Decisions

Key choices in building the Dimensional Fact Model and the Star Schema

01
Granularity
The fact table records each individual order_item — the finest granularity available. Enables per-product and per-seller analysis, impossible at the aggregated order level.
02
Pruning — customer_unique_id
Removed from the dimension: 99,441 distinct values for 99,441 customers makes the attribute non-aggregable. Only customer_state and city are retained.
03
Grafting — Geography
The zip_code_prefix (~19K values) is aggregated up to customer_state, preserving the geographic hierarchy while improving analysis readability.
04
Measure Additivity
price, freight_value: fully additive. delivery_lead_days: semi-additive (AVG/MAX/MIN only, not SUM). payment_value: semi-additive — order-level only, not item-level.
05
Double-Counting in Payments
For multi-item orders, payment_value is replicated for each row → R$19.23M vs R$15.38M (+25% overestimate). All payment analyses use the deduplicated value at order level.
06
Offline LLM — Privacy
Chose llama3.2:3b via Ollama over cloud APIs: zero data transmission, fully air-gapped execution, no cost, and reproducible results.

Results

Key Findings

The 6 main insights emerging from the full end-to-end pipeline

12 days
Median delivery lead time — 71% of orders delivered within 14 days (70K on time vs 28K slow). Northern states suffer up to 28 days vs SP's ~9.
R$ 13M
Total revenue · 98,666 orders — payment_value double-counting (+25%) corrected by deduplicating at order level before aggregating.
#1
health_beauty is the top revenue category, followed by watches_gifts and bed_bath_table. Credit card dominates payments by a wide margin.
>40%
São Paulo concentration — SP + RJ + MG account for 70%+ of all orders. Northern Brazil faces delivery times 3× longer (28+ days vs. SP's 8.7).
+191%
Explosive growth 2016→2017 — Platform expansion clearly visible. November 2017 Black Friday spike: over 8,000 items sold in a single month.
4 / 5
Median review score — 56,885 five-star reviews vs 10,786 one-star. Strongly right-skewed: most customers are satisfied, but 1-star reviews correlate with late deliveries.

Conclusions

Quality in,
quality out.

A rigorous DQA process — assisted by a local LLM — combined with correct dimensional modelling produces reliable analysis on a complex, multi-table real-world dataset.

Two planned extensions: macro-category mapping for products, and a bridge table for orders with multiple payment methods.

Three Takeaways
1
Data quality is a prerequisite
Without the DQA phase, the payment double-counting (+25%) and the 789 miskeyed review records would have silently corrupted every downstream analysis.
2
Geography explains everything
The SP–RJ–MG triangle dominates volume, revenue, and seller density. Northern states face 3× longer delivery times — a structural logistical gap, not a performance failure.
3
Growth is real — and fragile
+191% orders in one year, R$15.4M in revenue, 4.5/5 avg review. But Olist over-promises delivery windows: the 12-day buffer is a risk, not a feature.
